Common upload errors caught before submission

Amazon KDP and IngramSpark enforce strict mechanical rules on PDF files. Small export mistakes stop your book during automated file intake.

Unembedded fonts. PDF readers display local system fonts on your screen even when the typeface is not embedded in the file. Print machinery requires embedded fonts to rasterize glyphs accurately on physical paper.

Spine width mismatch. Paperback spine thickness depends on page count and paper thickness. An incorrect spine measurement shifts your cover artwork and causes cover rejection.

Margin and safe area errors. KDP requires minimum margins based on total page count. Page numbers or body text placed inside the live element margin boundary get flagged immediately.

Image resolution drops. Raster artwork below 300 DPI prints blurry and triggers automated quality warnings.

What it is honest about

When the tool cannot verify a detail, it reports unknown. It never reports a speculative pass. The IngramSpark spine width calculation is an estimate unless you provide the exact number from IngramSpark's template generator. Some limits come from files print facilities accepted in practice rather than published documentation. The checks were developed against accepted files from two published book editions. A passing report does not guarantee that a printer will accept your file.

How you use it

Preflight runs as a skill in your AI agent conversation. You supply the local path to your exported PDF, the printer name, trim dimensions and paper stock. The script inspects the file on your machine and outputs the table above. It makes no remote network calls. Your manuscript never leaves your hard drive.
